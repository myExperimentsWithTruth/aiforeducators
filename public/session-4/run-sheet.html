1d. Synthesis 6 min

  1. Paste this prompt into Gemini and press Enter. 
    CONTEXT: I am an Associate Professor of Genetics and Plant Breeding at GBPUAT,
Pantnagar. My strengths are marker-assisted selection and field-based phenotyping
in bread wheat. My region is the rice-wheat Tarai of Uttarakhand, North Western
Plains Zone, where wheat is sown late after paddy and grain filling meets rising
March temperatures, so the crop faces terminal heat stress. GBPUAT is a funded
AICRP wheat centre. I have already done the groundwork: target agencies and fit
(ICAR Extramural and AICRP for field-ready work, ANRF Advanced Research Grant for
mechanism-led genetics, DBT for molecular work); confirmed gaps from my own
Scholar reading (most heat-tolerance QTLs were mapped in durum or other
backgrounds and few are validated in late-sown Tarai bread wheat; seedling-stage
juvenile heat tolerance under early October sowing is under-studied; heat
tolerance is usually bred in isolation from the foliar blight that co-occurs in
the warm humid Tarai); funded landscape (adult-plant terminal-heat QTL mapping
looks crowded, early-sowing juvenile tolerance and stacked heat-plus-disease
selection look sparse). ROLE: Act as a senior agricultural research strategist
who helps faculty choose a thrust area before they write a proposal. TASK: Using
only the context I supplied, rank 3 to 4 candidate thrust areas at the
intersection of these agency priorities, these confirmed gaps, and my expertise
and location. Rank by feasibility for a single department over three years, not
ambition. For each, give a short title, then three one-liners: Priority, Feasible
here, Gap. Name the best-fit agency for each. Flag any claim I must verify.
FORMAT: a numbered ranked list, tight, with labelled one-liners and the agency
named for each item.
  2. Read item 1 out loud and check each of its one-liners against what you showed earlier: Priority against 1a, Gap against 1b, and Feasible here against the AICRP page.
  3. Read out one item from Gemini's "must verify" flags.

Theme 2: Agency language 0:38 to 1:05

2a. Read NICRA 6 min

  1. Open nicra-icar.in and go to its objectives page. Read the three objectives on screen.
    If it doesn't load: open the NICRA text in the box below and read it from there. It comes from NICRA's public description.
  2. Copy the NICRA text from the box below; you'll paste it into the next prompt.
    NICRA text to paste
    NICRA (National Innovations in Climate Resilient Agriculture) is a network project of ICAR, launched in February 2011, to enhance the resilience of Indian agriculture to climate change and climate variability through strategic research and technology demonstration.

Objectives:
1. To enhance the resilience of Indian agriculture covering crops, livestock and fisheries to climatic variability and climate change through development and application of improved production and risk management technologies.
2. To demonstrate site-specific technology packages on farmers' fields for adapting to current climate risks.
3. To enhance the capacity of scientists and other stakeholders in climate-resilient agricultural research and its application.

Components: Strategic Research (adaptation and mitigation; vulnerable districts; crop varieties and management practices); Technology Demonstration on farmers' fields in climatically vulnerable districts through KVKs in participatory mode; Sponsored and Competitive Grants to fill critical research gaps; Capacity Building of scientists and stakeholders.

Field modules: natural resource management, soil health, crop production, livestock. District Agricultural Contingency Plans; Climate Resilient Villages.

Recurring vocabulary: resilience, climate-resilient, climatic variability and climate change, adaptation and mitigation, vulnerable districts, adaptive capacity, site-specific technology packages, participatory mode, risk management, climatic aberrations, security of livelihood, self-reliant.

2b. Build the NICRA Alignment Skill 9 min

  1. Start a new Gemini chat. Paste this prompt with the NICRA text inserted, then press Enter. 
    CONTEXT: Below is the publicly stated description of ICAR's NICRA initiative
(National Innovations in Climate Resilient Agriculture): its objectives,
its four components, its vulnerable-district and on-farm demonstration
model, its four field modules, and its recurring vocabulary.
[paste NICRA text]
ROLE: Act as a research grant analyst who studies funding initiatives and
extracts what they reward.
TASK: Read the NICRA description and extract a structured profile of what
NICRA is looking for. Organise it under these headings: (a) priority
themes, (b) preferred vocabulary and phrasing, (c) what NICRA rewards
in a proposal, (d) what it treats as irrelevant or is indifferent to,
(e) common ways proposals overstate fit, so I can avoid them. Use only
what the pasted source supports. Do not invent priorities.
FORMAT: Five labelled sections matching (a) to (e). Bullet points. British
spellings. Plain prose, no promotional language.
  2. In the same chat, paste this prompt and press Enter. You don't need to paste anything into it, because Gemini still has the first answer in front of it. 
    CONTEXT: The NICRA profile you just extracted, above.
ROLE: Act as a prompt designer who writes compact, reusable rubrics.
TASK: Tighten this profile into a single self-contained "alignment skill" that
I can paste, unchanged, at the top of any future proposal-writing task for
NICRA. Add transformation rules for converting a plain research narrative into
NICRA-aligned language without inflating the science, and add a rule that every
aligned claim must name the specific NICRA priority it connects to. Keep only
what the profile supports.
FORMAT: A titled skill with these labelled sections: Priority themes; Preferred
vocabulary; What NICRA rewards; What NICRA ignores or penalises; Transformation
rules; Accountability check. British spellings. Plain prose.
  3. Copy Gemini's answer into a Google Doc named NICRA Alignment Skill. This is the reusable skill.
    If the answer is weak: use the ready-made v1 skill in section 4 of the alignment worked example.
    Room: 3 minutes to build their own skill.

2c. Apply the skill 9 min

  1. Start a new Gemini chat. Paste this prompt with the skill and the story inserted, then press Enter. 
    CONTEXT: Below is my NICRA Alignment Skill, followed by a plain research story
with no agency framing. [paste skill] [paste story]
ROLE: Act as an experienced agricultural grant writer applying the skill.
TASK: Rewrite the story so it aligns with NICRA's objectives and vocabulary,
following the skill's transformation rules exactly. Keep every claim faithful to
the story; add nothing the story does not support. Apply the skill's
accountability check to each aligned objective and outcome.
FORMAT: a short aligned narrative, then an Objectives list and an Expected
outcomes list, each numbered, with an 'Aligns with:' line under each item.
    Plain story to paste
    Wheat is the main winter crop for farmers across the Tarai belt of Uttarakhand. In recent years, many growers have noticed that the cool part of the season is getting shorter. When February and March turn warm too quickly, the crop does not get enough time to fill its grain, and yields drop. Farmers who sow late are hit hardest, because their crop meets the heat at exactly the wrong stage.

Our department has been collecting wheat lines that seem to cope better with this early heat. Some come from our own older crosses, and some from material we have gathered over the years. We plan to screen these lines carefully, measure how they behave when the temperature climbs during grain filling, and work out which traits let the better ones hold their yield. Once we know which lines perform, we want to grow them out on a few farmers' fields in the district to see how they do in real conditions.

If this works, farmers in the region would have wheat that is less likely to fail in a warm year, and we would understand more about what makes wheat tolerant to heat at that stage. We could then share seed of the best lines and keep improving them.
  2. Pick Objective 1 and do the two checks out loud. First, is the priority on its "Aligns with:" line a real NICRA priority? Check it against the 2a text. Second, is the claim actually in the plain story?
  3. Strike out any item that fails either check.
